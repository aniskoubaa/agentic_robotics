# The arm platform: a bench-mounted UR5e, and what it cost to make it work

Author: Prof. Anis Koubaa <anis.koubaa@gmail.com>
Date: 2026-08-25

A fourth platform, `arm/`, carrying the same three teaching layers as the
other three. It reuses the UR5e + Robotiq 2F-85 that RaiseBot already has on
its Husky, on a fixed pedestal, so the kinematic chain and the joint topics
are identical strings on both and everything transfers in either direction.

Seven packages, 12 executables:

| Package | Holds |
|---|---|
| `agr_arm_description` | pedestal + UR5e + Robotiq + two cameras + controllers |
| `agr_arm_worlds` | `agr_workcell`: bench, three blocks, a drop tray |
| `agr_arm_bringup` | launch files, topic bridge |
| `agr_arm_tools` | kinematics, `Arm` helper, three servers, `workspace` |
| `agr_arm_teleop` | `teleop_keyboard`, `camera_view` |
| `agr_arm_examples` | `01_read_joints` .. `06_pick_and_place` |
| `agr_arm_demos` | `diagnose`, `demo_pick_and_place` |

## Nine things that had to be measured rather than assumed

Each of these looked settled on paper and was wrong in the simulator.

### 1. One set of PID gains does not fit six joints

RaiseBot drives this arm with 1500/50/80 on all six. Measured here, that puts
the wrist joints in a limit cycle: `wrist_3` sat **0.11 rad** off target with
its velocity flipping between +2.5 and −2.5 rad/s forever, never settling.

Critical damping is `2·sqrt(p·J)`. `shoulder_lift` swings twenty kilograms of
arm; `wrist_3` turns a gripper about its own axis, maybe 0.01 kg·m². The
`d_gain` that is right for one is twenty times critical for the other, and an
over-damped loop amplifies the derivative of a quantised position signal into
exactly that buzz. Per-joint gains: 0.11 rad → under 0.005 rad.

### 2. `i_max` below the gravity load guarantees a steady-state error

A UR5e reaching out needs on the order of 80 N·m at the shoulder to hold
itself up. With the integral capped at 30, the proportional term must supply
the other 50 — which it can only do *by sitting at an error*. Measured droop
reaching for the bench: 0.028 rad, **32 mm** of tool error, enough to miss a
50 mm block entirely.

Raising `i_max` to just under `cmd_max` fixed the magnitude. Raising `i_gain`
fixed the *speed*: at `i_gain=400` the last 0.045 rad of every move took 6-9
seconds while the gross motion took 0.3-0.7 s.

### 3. Faster is not strictly better — the arm crashed into the bench

At `i_gain=2000` moves settled in 2.5-3 s, and a move from home straight to a
bench-level pose drove the gripper *into the table* and jammed there,
`wrist_2` stuck 0.29 rad out. Higher gains change how synchronously the joints
arrive, so the transient path changes, and there is no path planning here to
notice. Settled at `i_gain=1000`, which converges in 3.9-5.5 s and does not
crash — and the discipline of *hover, then descend* is documented everywhere
it matters.

### 4. A plain `<camera>` publishes on its `<topic>` **exactly**

`<topic>bench_camera</topic>` yields `/bench_camera` for the image and a
root-level `/camera_info` — not `/bench_camera/image` and
`/bench_camera/camera_info`. The bridge was configured for the latter, so the
ROS topic existed and never carried a single frame. `rgbd_camera` uses the
nested layout, which is why the wrist camera worked and the bench one did
not. Naming the topic `bench_camera/image` gives both cameras the same shape.

### 5. Where you mount a wrist camera matters more than how you aim it

Three attempts, all of which look right on paper:

1. Copied from RaiseBot — 0.07 m out along the tool's +x, looking down the
   tool axis. The Robotiq's palm fills the right half of every frame.
2. Out to 0.10 m and toed in to cross the tool axis at the grasp point. Better
   framing; a block directly under the gripper still completely hidden.
3. Offset along the tool's **+y** — *perpendicular* to the plane the fingers
   open in — and toed in. Works.

The reason is that the fingers open along ±x, so a camera offset along +x
looks straight down the line of one finger, and that finger is between the
lens and the object every single time.

### 6. Gazebo + DART will not hold a 50 mm cube between two rubber pads

Measured: close the Robotiq on a block and the knuckle sails past the block's
width to 0.66 rad while the block squirts out sideways and ends up 5 cm away.
No friction tuning fixes this reliably.

So a grasp here is a real kinematic constraint — a gz `DetachableJoint` per
block, welded and broken by `grasp_server`. Three consequences worth knowing:

- **`DetachableJoint` welds on load** and has no "start detached" option, so
  every block is stuck to the wrist from the moment the robot spawns.
  `grasp_server` breaks all of them at startup and restores the blocks to the
  positions declared in the world SDF, which it parses to find out.
- **Detaching is asynchronous.** A `set_pose` issued straight after a detach
  is still fighting a live weld and the block snaps back. The first version
  restored exactly one of three blocks — the last one, purely because it
  happened to be far enough behind the detach.
- **`parent_link` must be a link that survives sdformat's fixed-joint
  folding.** Everything from the flange out collapses into `ur5e_wrist_3_link`
  before the plugin sees it; naming the gripper base gets a plugin that
  silently does nothing.

### 7. Grab on the command, release on the measurement

The asymmetry is the difference between placing a block and firing it across
the bench.

Grabbing on the *commanded* knuckle angle means the weld forms before the pads
arrive, so a block cannot be knocked away by the grasp that is closing on it.

Releasing on the command would break the weld while the fingers are still
clamped — not resting, *pressing*, stalled 0.3 rad short with a 200 `p_gain`
behind them. Measured, the block came out **15 cm** from where it was placed.
Waiting for the measured opening means the pads let go before the constraint
does.

### 8. Reported joint velocity is not a usable "has it stopped" signal

At a genuine standstill — every joint within 0.0002 rad of target — `wrist_1`
reports |v| up to **0.26 rad/s** and the gripper knuckle reports a flat
0.5 rad/s that never changes at all. Those are noise and quantisation from the
physics engine. The *positions* over the same interval are rock steady, so
`Arm` settles on position stability across a 0.3 s window instead.

Related: thresholding position alone is a trap in the other direction. The
error decays smoothly through whatever value you pick, so a loose tolerance
returns while the arm is still visibly moving and every measurement you then
take is exactly your own tolerance. Both conditions are needed.

### 9. Your tool frame is not your fingertips

The pads reach **35 mm past** the tool centre point. Aim the TCP at a 50 mm
block's centre and the fingertips are exactly on the table it is sitting on;
the arm stalls and the move times out short with no explanation.

The mesh bounding box says 25 mm. The real number is 35 because a pad is 25 mm
*deep* as well as long, and that depth rotates with `wrist_3` — so which
corner is lowest depends on the grasp yaw, and commanding the mesh-derived
height stalled on some yaws and descended cleanly on others.
`kinematics.lowest_tcp_over()` takes the worst case.

## Two DDS discovery bugs, same shape, three places

`create_publisher()` returns instantly; discovery does not. A message
published before the subscriber is matched lands nowhere, with no error.

- `02_move_a_joint` published one command on the line after creating the
  publisher and reported "asked for +0.400, settled at −0.000" against a
  perfectly healthy robot. **Measured discovery time on this machine: up to
  5.7 s.**
- `demo_pick_and_place` published its scene reset the same way; the reset was
  lost, and the demo then picked the blocks up from wherever the previous run
  had left them — which still "works" and is not the demo anyone meant.
- `diagnose`'s first check sampled `/clock` for a fixed 2 s and twice reported
  "Gazebo is not running" against a simulator that was provably stepping.

All three now wait for the connection and say how long it took.

Note the contrast: `Arm.move_joints()` republishes at 20 Hz, so it never had
this problem. The examples that publish *once* — deliberately, to show that
the controller latches — are the ones that had to be fixed.

## One inherited comment that is wrong

`ground/raisebot_description/urdf/raisebot_gazebo.urdf.xacro` says
`<update_rate>` on the `JointStatePublisher` plugin "caps the publish
frequency at 50 Hz". It does not. gz-sim 8's `JointStatePublisher` accepts
exactly one parameter, `<topic>`: the library contains no reference to
`update_rate` at all, and the tag is parsed and ignored.

Verified against `libgz-sim8-joint-state-publisher-system.so.8.11.0` and
confirmed live — on the arm, `/joint_states` measures **998 Hz** against a
world stepping at 1000 Hz. The arm's URDF now says so and carries no fake cap.
The ground platform's file is left alone, as it is outside this change.

## Known limits

- **No path planning and no collision checking.** Every move is "solve IK,
  publish six numbers, wait". A pick is always hover-then-descend because the
  straight line between two IK solutions is straight in *joint* space and
  bulges in real space. `workspace` says what is reachable, which is a
  statement about six joint angles, not a promise that the path is clear.
- **Graspable objects are named in the robot description**, because
  `DetachableJoint` binds its child model at load time. Adding a block to the
  world means adding a line to `agr_arm_gazebo.urdf.xacro`.
- **`/joint_states` runs at the physics rate**, near 1 kHz. Anything printing
  per message must throttle itself.
- **A block dropped onto one already in the tray stays stacked.** That counts
  as success — the task is to clear the bench, not to arrange a single layer.
